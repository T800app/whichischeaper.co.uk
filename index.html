Shopping Guides & FAQs

Practical, plain-English guides to help you compare unit prices like a pro. Tap any question to open the answer.

Are the calculator results 100% accurate?

The mathematical comparison is precise, but the final result should be used as guidance only. The tool only knows the numbers you feed it.

It cannot account for real-world nuances, such as:

  • Quality/Ingredients: A premium brand might cost more per gram but taste better or last longer.
  • Drained Weight: A tin of beans has water in it. If you compare total weight instead of drained weight, the maths will be misleading.
  • Concentration: A concentrated squash or detergent makes more liquid when diluted. Comparing bottle sizes directly can be tricky.
  • Waste: A huge bag of potatoes is cheaper per kg, but if half of it rots, it was a false economy.
  • Special Offers: The tool doesn't automatically calculate "3-for-2" or "Buy One Get One Free" offers unless you input the final paid price and total quantity.
Why doesn't the calculator show the price per 100g?

Supermarkets use "price per 100g" on shelf labels to make comparison easier, but when you are standing in the aisle holding two different packs, doing the maths in your head is annoying. Most people just want to know: Which one do I buy, and how much money am I saving?

This tool does the complex 100g maths behind the scenes, but translates the result into plain English for you. It tells you exactly which product is the better deal based on the sizes you entered, and explains the real-world difference.

What do the blue and orange bars mean?

When you click "Compare Prices", a small visual bar appears inside the Product 1 card (blue) and Product 2 card (orange).

These bars visually represent the unit price of each product. The product with the shorter bar is mathematically cheaper per gram, per millilitre, or per item. It's a quick visual way to see the difference at a glance.

How does the Recent Comparisons feature work?

The tool automatically saves your last 3 calculations directly in your browser using local storage. This means you can look back at recent comparisons without re-entering the data.

Simply tap on a history item to instantly reload the data and see the result again. Your data is never sent to a server, and you can clear the history at any time.

How does the calculator determine the best value?

When you enter the price and size of two products, the tool first calculates the hidden unit price of both (price per gram, per millilitre, or per item) to find the mathematical winner.

Then, instead of just showing you confusing decimal numbers, it compares the actual amounts and prices you entered. It will tell you things like:

"You get 112 g more AND save £0.23."
or
"It costs £2.00 more upfront, but you get 500 g extra product."

This gives you a clear, real-world answer to decide which pack goes in your trolley.

Are bigger packs always better value?

Larger packs usually cost less per unit, but not always. Before buying the biggest size on the shelf, check these points:

  • Waste: will you use it before the best-before date? Spoiled food at any price is expensive.
  • Storage: bulky rice, toilet roll or pet food needs cupboard or freezer space.
  • Branding: some “family size” packs carry a premium price rather than a saving.
  • Offers: a half-price small pack can beat a full-price large pack on unit price.

Use this tool to compare the exact sizes, then buy the largest size you will realistically use.

Which units can this tool compare?

Grams (g), kilograms (kg), millilitres (ml), litres (L) and packs or individual items (each). Always compare within the same category: weight with weight (g and kg can be mixed), volume with volume (ml and L can be mixed), and packs/items with packs/items.

Can I compare two different brands?

Yes. The calculator compares the true value of any two products regardless of brand, which is the best way to see whether a premium brand is actually worth the extra cost.

Is the calculator free to use?

Yes, it is completely free, with no sign-up, no login and no app download. It works in the browser on mobile phones, tablets, laptops and desktop computers.

How to Use
Weight — Grams (g) or Kilograms (kg)

Enter the exact amount from the label. Example: type 500 and pick g for 500 grams, or type 1.5 and pick kg for 1.5 kilograms.

Volume — Millilitres (ml) or Litres (L)

Enter the exact volume. Example: type 750 and pick ml for 750 millilitres, or type 2 and pick L for 2 litres.

Quantity — Packs or Individual Items

Type the total number of items, then pick "pack / each". Example: type 6 and pick "pack / each" for a 6-pack of crisps.
